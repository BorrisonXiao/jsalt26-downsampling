0 · Background — data, model & pipeline

Standard LibriSpeech ASR, but the audio-to-LLM interface is learned rather than fixed: a small per-frame policy decides where an utterance breaks into segments, so the compression rate can adapt to the audio instead of pooling every k frames by hand (the fixed-rate baseline this report compares against below).

Pipeline

The segmenter's predicted boundaries are the dynamic downsampling: more boundaries → more, shorter audio tokens reaching the decoder; fewer boundaries → a lower kept-ratio ρ and higher compression. Source for this figure: make_pipeline_figure.py (also emits an editable .drawio).

1 · The collapse, and the fix

Teacher-forced NLL from an undertrained decoder plus a per-frame entropy bonus drove the boundary probabilities to a uniform mush: the argmax produced ~0 boundaries, the decoder saw ~1 token, WER hit 100%.

Diagnosis. Entropy pushed every p→0.5 (the rate loss constrains only the sum, not sharpness) → uniform p, empty argmax. And teacher-forced NLL under-rewards audio → RL pushed ρ down. Fix: drop the entropy bonus + warm the decoder longer (2→6 epochs).

1b · Revisiting the failure — what actually caused it

Now that we have non-collapsing runs to compare against, it's worth being precise about the mechanism — the failure history is actually two different collapses, not one, and the eventual fix changed two variables at once, so this is an honest post-mortem rather than a clean ablation.

Failure #1 (original run): a true absorbing state, not entropy-smearing. At epoch 3 both the argmax ρ and the expected ρ crash together, to ≈0.003 and ≈0.002 — not to a moderate shared value. The instrumented GRPO trace (grpo_trace.py) makes the mechanism exact: for every traced utterance the collapsed policy has mean-p = 0.000 and per-frame entropy = 0.0000, so all K=8 sampled segmentations are bit-for-bit identical — same ρ, same reward, reward std = 0.0000, advantage = +0.000 for every sample. GRPO's group-relative advantage has zero within-group variance to learn from once every sample in the group is the same point — the gradient isn't small, it is exactly zero, everywhere. (Compare the healthy cold-start trace: mean-p 0.05–0.23, entropy 0.06–0.16, reward std 0.22–2.60, per-sample ρ spread up to 2×.) That's the absorbing state, and it explains why ρ stayed pinned at exactly 1.90e-3 for epochs 4 through 10 with no further movement.

Failure #2 (A/B/C retry): a different collapse, caused by entropy, not the rate objective. Adding a floor (jointB) or a free band (jointC) worked exactly as designed — the expected ρ (Σp/T, the differentiable proxy) never crashes to zero, it stays right where the rate objective wants it. But the argmax ρ — what the decoder actually receives — collapses anyway, within one or two epochs, in both rate objectives (a soft floor and a hard free-band are structurally different, yet produce the identical divergence signature). That commonality points at the one thing neither rate objective constrains: sharpness. Per-frame Bernoulli entropy is maximized at p=0.5, and the entropy bonus (still on here) pushes every frame toward it; the rate loss only constrains the sum Σp, never the shape of the per-frame distribution. So the policy can satisfy "correct average rate" while every single frame sits uniformly below the argmax threshold (p>0.5) — expected count healthy, decoded count empty. (jointA, a two-sided pin — in principle the most collapse-resistant of the three — was killed mid-epoch-3 once the same pattern was already clear in its siblings, so we don't have its numbers, but nothing in the mechanism above is specific to jointB or jointC's rate objective.)
A separate, third observation: the reward pointed the wrong way. Within jointB's own run, as expected ρ fell 0.184→0.119→0.077 across epochs 3–5, the teacher-forced NLL reward improved (−3.01→−2.93→−2.84) — the gradient the segmenter received was actively rewarding fewer audio tokens. Consistent with an undertrained (2-epoch) decoder leaning on its text prior rather than the audio: noisy under-trained audio conditioning hurts more than it helps. This is a genuinely separate problem from the entropy/sharpness mechanism above — it's about the sign of the gradient, not its shape.

So which fix mattered? The runs that finally didn't collapse (nll_frozen/nll_mt/cer_mt) changed two things at once — dropped entropy to exactly 0 and extended warmup 2→6 epochs — so this isn't a controlled experiment and we can't say with certainty which was necessary. Our read of the evidence: dropping entropy is the better-supported primary fix for the argmax-collapse specifically — it's a direct, within-run, mechanism-level explanation (p→0.5 is a plain mathematical property of the entropy bonus, not something that depends on decoder quality) that reproduces identically across two structurally different rate objectives. The longer warmup targets the separate reward-misdirection problem above, and plausibly also helps sharpness indirectly — a better-trained decoder should give higher-SNR, more decisive per-frame reward gradients that can outcompete entropy's smoothing pull — but no run isolates "entropy-off/short-warmup" or "entropy-on/long-warmup", so that half of the story is inferred, not proven.

The later corrected on-policy sweep resolves this: CNN CER genuinely trains to 6.67±0.41 / 11.31±0.39 WER, but still trails NLL co-training and costs about 3× more.
