On-policy prefix distillation

Adapt the decoder to the shorter, variable audio prefixes produced by the current segmenter, while a frozen oracle-char decoder anchors the text distribution. The first study freezes the segmenter so the decoder effect is measured without boundary drift.

The proposal makes sense, but its scope should be precise. It can train the projection and LoRA decoder to handle lower-rate prefixes. It does not backpropagate through hard segmentation, so GRPO remains responsible for updating the segmenter.

1 · Motivation

The working hypothesis is a decoder input-distribution mismatch, not simply a lack of decoder capacity.

Table 1. The learned system is nearly tied with the oracle-char reference on test-clean at a lower kept ratio, but its larger test-other gap leaves room for better robustness to shorter and noisier prefixes.

Current training only partly covers the inference distribution. Decoder CE uses one deterministic segmentation. GRPO explores sampled segmentations, but their decoder forwards are detached and become rewards rather than decoder training examples. The decoder therefore does not learn directly from the full prefix distribution induced by the segmenter.

This follows the general motivation of on-policy language-model distillation and DAgger-style training under model-induced inputs: train under the distribution the system itself creates, instead of only under a fixed reference distribution.

Figure 1. Oracle-prefix teacher and learned-prefix student forwards.

For transcript positions M, the student loss is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{decoder}} &= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}, \\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} &= -\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}} \log p_S\!\left(y_t\mid z_{\mathrm{sample}},y_{<t}\right), \\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}} &= T^2\!\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}} \operatorname{KL}\!\left( p_T^{(T)}\!\left(\cdot\mid z_{\mathrm{char}},y_{<t}\right) \,\middle\|\, p_S^{(T)}\!\left(\cdot\mid z_{\mathrm{sample}},y_{<t}\right) \right). \end{aligned} \]

M is the set of unmasked transcript-token positions; z_char and z_sample are the oracle-character and sampled learned acoustic prefixes; T is the distillation temperature. The teacher distribution is the first argument of the forward KL.

Evaluation sequence

1

Fix the boundary sets

Calibrate the frozen segmenter to approximately ρ={0.29, 0.23, 0.20} on dev-clean. Reuse identical boundaries for every decoder arm.

2

Train three seeds

Use seeds 3407/3408/3409, matched update counts, and the same checkpoint rule.

3

Test the interaction

Compare sample vs. greedy, KD vs. no KD, and whether the KD gain grows as the prefix gets shorter.

Most diagnostic result: sampled CE+KD holds WER better than sampled CE as ρ moves from about 0.29 toward 0.20, while the exact same boundaries are used. That would directly support decoder prefix-rate robustness rather than improved boundary placement.

4 · Joint-RL extension

Only after the frozen-segmenter study identifies the decoder objective should both components be trained together.

Recommended first joint objective

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{decoder}} &= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{KD}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}, \\ R_{\mathrm{segmenter}} &= -\operatorname{NLL}_{S} - \lambda_{\rho}\mathcal{P}_{\rho}. \end{aligned} \]

Keep these roles separate first. The decoder learns from sampled prefixes; the segmenter receives the existing GRPO update using R_segmenter. Here NLL_S is the detached student transcript loss and P_rho penalizes deviation from the current kept-ratio target. Use a gradual rate schedule: 0.29 → 0.25 → 0.23 → 0.20.

Optional later ablation: use detached teacher–student KL as part of the GRPO reward. Do not include it initially: the segmenter could learn prefixes that are easy to imitate rather than prefixes that retain the information needed for ASR.

Table 4. Main confounds and safeguards.

A null result would also be useful. If sampled CE and sampled CE+KD do not beat deterministic CE at matched boundaries, the remaining gap is less likely to be decoder prefix-rate bias. Boundary information loss or boundary placement then becomes the stronger explanation.
